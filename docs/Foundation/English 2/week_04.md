## Argumentative Writing

**Argumentative writing** presents a reasoned position on an issue using **evidence, multiple perspectives, and persuasive language**.

### Key Elements

| Element                    | Purpose                                                                    |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Content & Evidence**     | Support arguments with data, research, examples, and relevant observations |
| **Multiple Perspectives**  | Consider both sides or different viewpoints before reaching a conclusion   |
| **Transition Markers**     | Connect and organize different parts of the argument                       |
| **Adjectives & Adverbs**   | Add emphasis and indicate comparison, preference, or degree                |
| **Quantifiers**            | Indicate the size or significance of a group or claim                      |
| **Rhetorical Expressions** | Direct the reader's attention to important points                          |

### Useful Language

* **Introducing perspectives:** *on the one hand, on the other hand*
* **Expressing viewpoint:** *in my view, as far as I am concerned*
* **Comparing:** *more, less, most, least*
* **Quantifying:** *many, majority, half*
* **Directing attention:** *before we conclude, in totality*

> **Key idea:** Strong argumentative writing combines **substantial content + balanced perspectives + strategic language**.

## Structuring an Argument

A strong argumentative piece requires a **clear structure, appropriate syntax, and balanced reasoning**.

### Structure

* A **thesis/position** may appear at the beginning, middle, or end, depending on the intended effect.
* Arguments should remain **concise, engaging, and balanced**.
* Consider opposing perspectives rather than presenting a purely biased argument.

### Syntax and Language

**Complex sentences** can connect ideas, contrast viewpoints, and establish conditions.

Common connectors:

* **while**
* **whereas**
* **if...then**
* **although**
* **because**

> *While global chains offer lower prices, local businesses may provide greater community value.*

Both **statistical evidence** and **anecdotal evidence** can support an argument, depending on the context.

### Writing Process

> **Draft → Revise → Rewrite**

Repeated writing and revision improve the **clarity, quality, and depth** of an argument.

> **Key idea:** Effective argumentative writing is not just about having a position; it is about **structuring, supporting, and refining that position for the audience**.

## Writing Lab Manuals

A **lab manual** is a technical document that provides **precise, clear, and logically ordered instructions** for carrying out a process.

### Core Principles

| Principle                 | Meaning                                                                |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Nomenclature**          | Use precise names for tools, materials, and ingredients                |
| **Procedural verbs**      | Use specific action verbs such as *boil, simmer, stir,* and *roast*    |
| **Sequence**              | Present steps in the correct order                                     |
| **Duration**              | Specify how long an action should continue                             |
| **Observable indicators** | Describe changes in colour, smell, form, etc. that indicate completion |

### Professionalism

Even experts can make mistakes through **overconfidence, distraction, or assumptions**. Precise procedures help reduce human error and improve safety.

### The Three Pillars

> **Process + Sequence + Duration**

A good procedural manual tells the reader **what to do, in what order, and for how long**, using precise and observable instructions.

## Persuasive Writing: Ethics and Techniques

Persuasive writing uses **language, evidence, and presentation choices** to influence how readers think or make decisions.

### Common Manipulation Techniques

| Technique                       | How it can mislead                                                                         |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Selective quantitative data** | Percentages are presented without absolute numbers or sample size                          |
| **Vague language**              | Terms such as *high quality* or *strong preference* lack precise definitions               |
| **Selective sampling**          | Describing a particular group of respondents can create an impression of broader authority |

### Critical Analysis

When reading persuasive writing, ask:

* **How large is the sample?**
* **Who was surveyed?**
* **What timeframe is being discussed?**
* **Are important terms clearly defined?**
* **Does the evidence actually support the conclusion?**

### Ethics of Persuasion

Persuasion itself is not necessarily unethical. The ethical concern is whether language is used **transparently and honestly** or deliberately to **mislead and manipulate**.

> **Key idea:** Effective critical reading requires a **filter** that separates genuine evidence from persuasive presentation.

## Reflective Essay Writing

A reflective essay explores complex questions that may not have a single objective answer. **Systematic preparation, cohesion, and revision** improve clarity and quality.

### 1. Preparation: Synopsis

A **synopsis** is a brief overview or roadmap of the essay.

Include:

* Definitions and dimensions of the topic
* Logical arguments and evidence
* Relevant personal anecdotes
* Multiple perspectives

> **Synopsis = map of the essay before drafting**

### 2. 30-Minute Writing Plan

| Stage        |   Time | Focus                                  |
| ------------ | -----: | -------------------------------------- |
| **Synopsis** |  5 min | Plan points, evidence, examples        |
| **Drafting** | 20 min | Develop ideas and maintain flow        |
| **Revision** |  5 min | Correct errors and improve conciseness |

### 3. Cohesion and Flow

Use **cohesive devices** to connect ideas and paragraphs:

* **Discourse markers:** however, therefore, furthermore, consequently
* **Pronouns & prepositions:** link ideas without unnecessary repetition
* **Thematic connection:** each paragraph should logically follow the previous one

### 4. Revision

Revision is essential because hurried writing can contain grammatical, spelling, and punctuation errors.

During revision:

* Correct language errors.
* Remove redundant words.
* Improve clarity and flow.
* Ensure the essay stays within the word limit.

### 5. Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall                  | Problem                                      |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- |
| **Partisan view**        | Ignores opposing perspectives or constraints |
| **Irrelevant anecdotes** | Personal examples do not support the topic   |
| **Lack of focus**        | Ideas drift away from the question           |
| **Poor cohesion**        | Sentences or paragraphs feel disconnected    |

> **Key Principle:** Plan → draft → connect ideas → revise.

# Contrast Words

Contrast words are used to **link opposing ideas or expectations** effectively in English.

## 1. Core Contrast Words

### But

* The simplest and most common contrast word.
* Links a result that conflicts with an initial expectation.

> I wanted to go, **but** I was too tired.

### However

* Serves the same function as **but**, but is significantly more **formal**.
* Preferred in written and formal communication.
* In formal settings, avoid contractions:

  * *did not* rather than *didn't*

> I wanted to go. **However**, I was too tired.

**Key:** But → common/informal; However → formal/written.

## 2. Although, Though and Even Though

### Although

* Primarily used in **formal contexts and writing**.
* Can appear at the beginning or middle of a sentence.

### Though

* Informal, shortened version of **although**.
* Commonly used in speech.

### Even Though

* A stronger version of **although**.
* Adds **emotional weight** and emphasizes that an expectation was not met, often expressing disappointment.

When these expressions begin a sentence, the following phrase often receives **additional focus**.

**Key:** Although → formal; Though → informal; Even though → stronger emphasis.

## 3. Despite and In Spite Of

* **Despite** and **in spite of** are interchangeable.
* Unlike **although/even though**, they are followed by **noun phrases or verb phrases**, not independent clauses.

> **Despite her objection**, they continued.

> **In spite of the rain**, we went outside.

Incorrect:

> Despite she objected.

Correct:

> Despite **her objection**.

## 4. Common Error

**Never use “despite of.”**

Use either:

* **despite**
* **in spite of**

### Key Takeaways

| Expression      | Usage                        |
| --------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **but**         | Common contrast              |
| **however**     | Formal contrast              |
| **although**    | Formal contrast              |
| **though**      | Informal contrast            |
| **even though** | Stronger/emphatic contrast   |
| **despite**     | Followed by noun/verb phrase |
| **in spite of** | Followed by noun/verb phrase |

**Remember:**
> **Although + independent clause**
> **Despite/In spite of + noun ph**

# Morphology

**Morphology** is the study of the **internal structure of words** and the processes by which words are formed.

## 1. The Core Unit: Morpheme

A **morpheme** is the **smallest indivisible unit of language** that carries a distinct meaning or serves a grammatical function.

A morpheme cannot be broken down further without losing its core semantic or functional integrity.

## 2. Classification of Morphemes

### Free Morphemes

Can stand alone as independent words.

**Lexical (Open Class)**

* Carry substantive content.
* Include **nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs**.
* Called *open class* because languages continuously create or borrow new words.
* Examples: *mouse, window*.

**Grammatical (Closed Class)**

* Functional words such as **articles, prepositions, conjunctions, auxiliary verbs**.
* Called *closed class* because the set is relatively stable and rarely accepts new additions.

### Bound Morphemes

* Cannot exist in isolation.
* Must be attached to a **root or base word**.
* Primarily consist of **affixes**.

## 3. Affixation

Affixes are classified according to their position relative to the root:

| Type       | Position        | Example                                         |
| ---------- | --------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Prefix** | Beginning       | *un-*happy                                      |
| **Suffix** | End             | friend*ship*                                    |
| **Infix**  | Within the root | Rare in English; common in some other languages |

## 4. Derivational vs. Inflectional Affixes

### Derivational Affixes

* Change the **category or fundamental meaning** of the root.
* Create new vocabulary items.
* The process is highly productive.

> nation → nationalize

Here, **-ize** creates a verb from the noun *nation*.

### Inflectional Affixes

* Serve a **grammatical purpose**.
* Show features such as plurality, tense, possession, or comparison.
* **Do not change the word's basic category or meaning.**

### The 8 Standard English Inflections

| No. | Inflection   | Function                   |
| --- | ------------ | -------------------------- |
| 1   | **-s / -es** | Third-person singular verb |
| 2   | **-s / -es** | Plural noun                |
| 3   | **-'s**      | Possessive/Genitive        |
| 4   | **-ed**      | Past tense                 |
| 5   | **-en**      | Past participle            |
| 6   | **-ing**     | Progressive/Continuous     |
| 7   | **-er**      | Comparative                |
| 8   | **-est**     | Superlative                |

## 5. Word Formation and Productivity

Language is dynamic and constantly evolves through:

* **Semantic Extension** — existing words acquire new meanings.

  * *window* → computer interface
* **Borrowing** — adopting words from other languages.
* **Morphological Derivation** — using affixes to create new entries in the lexicon.

### Key Takeaways

* **Morphology** = study of word structure and formation.
* **Morpheme** = smallest meaningful/functional unit.
* **Free morpheme** = can stand alone.
* **Bound morpheme** = must attach to another form.
* **Derivational affix** = creates a new word/category or changes meaning.
* **Inflectional affix** = expresses grammar without changing basic category/meaning.
* English has **8 standard inflectional markers**.
* Word formation occurs through **semantic extension, borrowing, and derivation**.

# English Vocabulary - II

Word formation is the process by which new **lexical units** are created, modified, or derived to meet the evolving needs of communication.

## 1. Core Concepts

### Morpheme

A **morpheme** is the **minimal meaningful unit of language**. It may be a lexical root or a structural element carrying a specific grammatical function.

### Rule-Governed Formation

Word creation is **not arbitrary**. It follows specific patterns and structures unique to a language.

## 2. Word Formation Processes

### 1. Clipping (Shortening)

Reducing a longer word to a shorter form while maintaining its original meaning.

* **Back Clipping:** removes the end.

  * *examination → exam*
  * *gymnasium → gym*
* **Fore Clipping:** removes the beginning.

  * *telephone → phone*
  * *parachutes → chutes*
* **Complex/Mid-Clipping:** removes middle segments or parts of a compound.

  * *influenza → flu*
  * *advertisement + editorial → advertorial*

### 2. Acronyms

Acronyms are formed using the **initial letters of a phrase** and are often pronounced as independent words.

> NATO, laser, scuba

### 3. Blending

Combining parts of **two existing independent words** to create a new term.

> breakfast + lunch → **brunch**

> cybernetic + organism → **cyborg**

### 4. Back Formation

Creating a new word by removing an **actual or perceived affix** from an existing word.

> resurrection → **resurrect**

### 5. Borrowing

Adopting words from another language due to **cultural or historical contact**.

Borrowing is a **two-way phenomenon**.

> English: *jungle* ← Hindi

> Hindi: *road*, *school* ← English

### 6. Coinage

Inventing **entirely new words**, often based on corporate names or proprietary technologies.

> Xerox, Kleenex, Kodak

### 7. Compounding

Joining **two or more independent words** to form a new word with a distinct meaning.

> lip + stick → **lipstick**

> tea + pot → **teapot**

> baby + sit → **babysit**

### 8. Inflection

Adding suffixes to change the **grammatical form** of a word without altering its core meaning.

> go → going, went, gone

> fast → faster, fastest

### 9. Derivation

Using **prefixes or suffixes** to create new words or change the word category.

> kind → kindness

> modern → modernize

## Key Takeaways

| Process            | Main Idea                     | Example                  |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| **Clipping**       | Shortening a word             | examination → exam       |
| **Acronym**        | Initial letters form a word   | NATO                     |
| **Blending**       | Parts of two words combine    | brunch                   |
| **Back Formation** | Remove actual/perceived affix | resurrection → resurrect |
| **Borrowing**      | Adopt from another language   | jungle                   |
| **Coinage**        | Invent a new word             | Kodak                    |
| **Compounding**    | Join independent words        | lipstick                 |
| **Inflect**        |                               |                          |

# Connecting Ideas in English

**Connectors** help link ideas so that sentences form a cohesive, logical **discourse** rather than isolated statements. They improve **clarity, confidence, and flow** in speech and writing.

## 1. Core Purpose of Connectors

Connectors act as **“greasing agents” for language**.

Effective use of connectors:

* **Increases clarity** — helps the audience follow the line of reasoning.
* **Boosts confidence** — makes language sound natural and effortless.
* **Improves flow** — prevents the monotony of simple, disconnected sentences.

## 2. Categories of Discourse Markers

### Causality and Result

Connect a condition or cause to its outcome.

> **As a result, consequently, therefore, hence, thus**

### Contrast and Concession

Introduce opposing or surprising facts.

> **However, nevertheless, on the other hand, even though, on the contrary, even so**

### Addition and Expansion

Build upon a previous point.

> **In addition, furthermore, similarly, likewise, moreover**

### Time and Sequence

Connect events chronologically.

> **Meanwhile, at the same time, while, whenever**

## 3. Sentence Structure and Grammar

### Intra-sentential Connectors

Connect clauses **within a single sentence**.

> **While I was waiting,** I had a cup of coffee.

### Inter-sentential Connectors

Connect ideas **between separate sentences**.

> The AC was off. **As a result,** we were sent home.

### Punctuation with However

When **however** begins a new sentence, it is followed by a comma.

> **However,** it is not the only way.

## 4. Practical Mastery

### Active Analysis

Take a **five-minute audio/video sample** and identify the discourse markers used. Observe how they shape the speaker's argument.

### Personal Integration

Do not merely memorize lists. **Write or speak practice sentences** using each connector so that they become part of your natural language repertoire.

### Key Takeaways

* Connectors create **cohesion and logical flow**.
* **Causality/result** → therefore, consequently, as a result.
* **Contrast/concession** → however, nevertheless, even though.
* **Addition** → moreover, furthermore, in addition.
* **Time/sequence** → meanwhile, while, whenever.
* **Intra-sentential** → connects clauses within one sentence.
* **Inter-sentential** → connects ideas across sentences.
* Practice through **active analysis + personal integration**.

# Innovation in Vocabulary

English vocabulary is **dynamic** and continuously evolves through socio-cultural change, borrowing, creative word formation, and digital media.

## 1. Socio-Cultural Context and Language Use

Vocabulary building is not simply about collecting words. It is shaped by the **society and culture** in which a language is used.

* **Contextual Dependence:** Word choice depends on whether the setting is **formal or informal**.
* **Linguistic Competence:** Understanding what is possible within a language and how different domains influence word usage.

## 2. Classical Word Formation: Prefixes and Suffixes

English frequently borrows from **Greek and Latin** to create high-cognition, low-frequency words, particularly in **academic and technical fields**.

### Prefixes

| Prefix     | Meaning | Examples                    |
| ---------- | ------- | --------------------------- |
| **aero-**  | air     | aerobic, aeronautics        |
| **geo-**   | earth   | geography, geopolitical     |
| **hyper-** | excess  | hyperactive, hypersensitive |

### Suffixes

| Suffix    | Meaning  | Examples              |
| --------- | -------- | --------------------- |
| **-cide** | killer   | insecticide, homicide |
| **-ism**  | act of   | journalism, feminism  |
| **-logy** | study of | biology, geology      |

## 3. Language Borrowing and Dynamism

English is highly **dynamic** because it remains in contact with numerous languages, allowing extensive borrowing.

| Source               | Examples borrowed into English |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **Celtic**           | basket, clan                   |
| **Scandinavian**     | sky, window                    |
| **Indian languages** | karma, jungle, avatar, saree   |

Language borrowing reflects **cultural and historical contact** between language communities.

## 4. Contemporary Innovation: Instilingo

**Instilingo** refers to the specific code/slang used by students at **IIT Madras**.

It represents a blend of:

* Shortening
* Borrowing from regional languages such as Tamil, Malayalam and Hindi
* English

Examples:

* **Insti** → Institute + lingo → language/code
* **Funda** → shortening of *fundamental*
* **Gyan** → used ironically to mean unnecessary talk

## 5. Digital Media and New Vocabulary

Information technology and social media have introduced **new terms** and redefined existing words.

### New Terms

> Tweet, Insta, blog, unfriend

### Acronyms Used as Words

* **DP** → Display Picture
* **ASAP** → As soon as possible
* **LOL** → Laugh out loud
* **RIP** → Rest in peace

### Key Takeaways

* Vocabulary evolves with **society, culture and technology**.
* Word choice is **context-dependent**.
* Greek and Latin roots contribute heavily to **academic and technical vocabulary**.
* English expands through **borrowing** from other languages.
* Contemporary communities create localized vocabulary such as **Instilingo**.
* Digital media continuously creates and popularizes **new words and acronyms**.

# Advanced English Vocabulary — Loanwords

English vocabulary contains many **loanwords** borrowed from other languages. These advanced, low-frequency terms are particularly useful in **formal, legal, academic, and professional communication**.

## 1. Latin Loanwords

| **Ad hoc**         | Created for a specific purpose or case; temporary               |
| **Pro rata**       | Proportionately calculated                                      |
| **Modus operandi** | A specific way of operating or doing something                  |

## 2. French Loanwords

| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |

## Key Takeaways

* **Loanwords** are words adopted from other languages.

* **Latin** → formal/legal expressions.

* **Italian** → food, culture and intense situations.

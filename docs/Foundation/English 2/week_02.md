## Adverbs: A quick review

- An adverb is a word/a set of words that modifies verbs, adjectives, and
other adverbs. It expresses when, where, and how an action is performed
or indicates the quality or degree of the action.

**Example**:
- Shelly is always ready for help.
- He loved her very much.
- Time is running out fast.
- I gave that willingly.


### Types of Adverbs

- **Adverb Phrases & Clauses**: These are groups of words that function as adverbs to provide additional detail to a sentence.
- **Adverbs of Manner**: These answer "how" an action is performed, often ending in -ly (e.g., slowly, carefully).
- **Adverbs of Degree**: These express the intensity or extent of an action, answering "how much" (e.g., thoroughly, completely).
- **Adverbs of Place & Direction**: These indicate the location or path of an action, answering "where" (e.g., through the jungle, upstairs).
- **Adverbs of Time & Frequency**: These answer "when" or "how often" an action takes place (e.g., yesterday, occasionally).
- **Sentence Adverbs**: These modify the entire sentence and typically appear at the beginning to express the speaker's viewpoint (e.g., hopefully, apparently).
- **Conjunctive Adverbs**: These serve as connectors between independent clauses, establishing transitions or relationships between ideas (e.g., nonetheless, consequently)

## Tense, Aspect & Verb Patterns

### Tense

**Tense** indicates **when an action or event occurs**.

* **Present** — occurs now or generally.
* **Past** — occurred in the past.
* **Future** — will occur later.

> **Tense = time of the action**

### Aspect

**Aspect** describes the **nature or state of an action**, such as whether it is habitual, ongoing, completed, or continuing over a period.

| Aspect                       | Meaning                                                    | Example                                   |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **Indefinite / Habitual**    | General, repeated, or habitual action                      | *She **works** every day.*                |
| **Continuous / Progressive** | Action in progress                                         | *She **is working** now.*                 |
| **Perfect**                  | Completed action                                           | *She **has worked** here before.*         |
| **Perfect Continuous**       | Action continuing over a period, with emphasis on duration | *She **has been working** for two hours.* |

> **Tense = when**
> **Aspect = how the action unfolds**

### Verb Patterns

| Type                | Pattern                            | Example                           |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **Regular verbs**   | Usually form the past with **-ed** | walk → walked                     |
| **Irregular verbs** | Do not follow a fixed pattern      | go → went, sing → sang, put → put |

**Key point:** Irregular verbs must generally be learned individually because their past forms may involve vowel changes, no change, or completely different forms.

## Structure & functions of passives in english


### Passive Voice

**Active voice** focuses on the **doer (agent)** of an action, while **passive voice** focuses on the **receiver (theme)** of the action.

#### Formation

> **Passive = be + past participle (V3)**

When changing active to passive:

* The **direct object** of the active sentence becomes the **subject**.
* The original subject/agent can be included using **by**.
* The tense is retained through the form of **be**.

**Active:** *The chef cooked the meal.*
**Passive:** *The meal was cooked by the chef.*

#### When is Passive Voice Used?

* **Suppression:** When the agent is unknown, unimportant, or deliberately concealed.
* **Emphasis:** To focus on the action or its receiver rather than the doer.
* **Generalization:** Common in formal announcements and general statements where the agent is irrelevant.

#### Important Rules

| Rule                      | Explanation                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Transitive verbs only** | Only verbs that take an object can normally form passives.                                                        |
| **Object → Subject**      | The active sentence's direct object becomes the passive subject.                                                  |
| **Agent is optional**     | The agent can be omitted or introduced with **by**.                                                               |
| **Double objects**        | With direct and indirect objects, either may sometimes become the passive subject, depending on the construction. |

> **Active = focus on the doer**
> **Passive = focus on the receiver/action**


## Some important concepts in adjectives

### Adjectives

**Adjectives** modify nouns by describing their qualities, characteristics, or states.

### 1. Identifying Adjectives

Adjectives can often be recognized by common suffixes:

| Suffix            | Examples               |
| ----------------- | ---------------------- |
| **-able / -ible** | comfortable, invisible |
| **-al**           | educational, gradual   |
| **-ful**          | powerful, thoughtful   |
| **-ic / -ical**   | problematic, magical   |
| **-ive**          | selective, informative |
| **-less**         | harmless, careless     |
| **-ous**          | delicious, dangerous   |
| **-some**         | awesome, handsome      |

### 2. Functions and Placement

| Type                 | Position / Function                                   | Example                        |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **Attributive**      | Before the noun                                       | *an **old** car*               |
| **Predicative**      | After a verb; acts as a subject complement            | *The room is **clean**.*       |
| **Noun as modifier** | A noun modifies another noun to indicate type/purpose | *dinner table*, *music system* |

### 3. Positional Meaning

Some adjectives change meaning depending on their position.

| Adjective   | Attributive                                  | Predicative                  |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **late**    | *her **late** husband* → deceased            | *You are **late*** → delayed |
| **certain** | *certain reasons* → some unspecified reasons | *I am **certain*** → sure    |

### 4. Ordering

The basic pattern is:

> **Determiner + Adjective + Noun**
> *the dirty room*

With degree adverbs:

> **Article + Degree Adverb + Adjective + Noun**
> *a very cold day*

**Quite** and **rather** can occur before the article:

> *quite an expensive item*
> *rather an unusual idea*

> **Key idea:** Adjective **form, position, and order** can affect how it functions and what it means.

## Degree Adverbs

**Degree adverbs** express the **intensity or extent** of a quality, condition, or action.

Examples: **extremely, really, almost, completely, fairly, rather, slightly, too, totally, very much**

### Very vs. Too

| **Very**                | **Too**                                    |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Indicates a high degree | Indicates more than necessary or desirable |
| *It is **very hot**.*   | *It is **too hot** to stay.*               |

### Very vs. Very Much

| Context                                    | Use           | Example                                 |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Adjectives / adverbs                       | **very**      | *very difficult*                        |
| Certain verbs expressing feelings/opinions | **very much** | *I **very much** appreciate it.*        |
| **-ed / -ing** participle adjectives       | **very**      | *very disturbed*, *very disappointing*  |
| Passive + past participle                  | **very much** | *The highway was **very much needed**.* |

> **Very** normally modifies adjectives/adverbs; **very much** can modify certain verbs and passive constructions.

### Gradable vs. Non-Gradable Adjectives

| Type             | Meaning                        | Common Modifiers        | Example                       |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------ | ----------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Gradable**     | Can exist in different degrees | very, extremely, fairly | *very cold*, *extremely cold* |
| **Non-gradable** | Represents an absolute state   | absolutely, completely  | *completely finished*         |

### Quite

**Quite** has two important uses:

* With **gradable adjectives:** indicates a significant but usually less-than-very degree.

  * *quite difficult*
* With **non-gradable adjectives:** can mean **completely/totally**.

  * *quite impossible*

### Collocations

Certain degree adverbs naturally combine with particular adjectives:

* **extremely effective**
* **hugely successful**
* **deeply concerned**
* **highly unlikely**

> **Key idea:** The correct degree adverb depends on the **word being modified** and whether the adjective is **gradable or non-gradable**.

## Adverbs

**Adverbs** provide additional information about actions, qualities, or entire statements. Advanced adverbs can also express the speaker's **attitude, viewpoint, or focus**.

### Comment Adverbs

Express the speaker's **attitude, opinion, judgment, or degree of certainty** about a situation.

Examples:

* **Likelihood:** apparently, certainly, definitely, obviously
* **Attitude/opinion:** frankly, honestly, surprisingly
* **Judgment:** bravely, foolishly, wisely, kindly

They can occur at the beginning, middle, or end of a sentence.

> **Honestly**, I don't agree.
> I **honestly** don't agree.

### Viewpoint Adverbs

Show the **perspective or domain** from which a statement should be understood.

Examples: **biologically, financially, morally, politically, technically**

> **Financially**, the decision was a disaster.

> **Viewpoint adverb = “In what respect?”**

### Focus Adverbs

Highlight a **specific part of a sentence** and can change the meaning depending on their position.

Common focus adverbs: **only, even, alone**

> My brother has **only brought books**.
> → Focuses on **books**.

> **Only my brother** has brought books.
> → Focuses on **my brother**.

> **Key idea:** The position of a focus adverb determines what part of the sentence receives emphasis.

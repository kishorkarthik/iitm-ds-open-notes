## Sentence, Clause & Complex Sentences

### 1. Clause

A **clause** is a group of words containing a **subject + verb**.

### Independent Clause

An **independent clause** expresses a complete thought and can stand alone as a sentence.

> The students studied.

- Subject → `The students`
- Verb → `studied`
- Can stand alone → **Yes**

### Subordinate Clause

A **subordinate clause** cannot normally stand alone. It depends on another clause for its meaning.

> because the students studied

It contains a subject and verb, but does not express a complete thought by itself.

---

### 2. Compound vs Complex Sentences

### Compound Sentence

A **compound sentence** contains two or more independent clauses joined together.

> I studied, and I passed the exam.

- `I studied` → Independent clause
- `I passed the exam` → Independent clause
- Connector → `and`

**Structure:**

`Independent Clause + Conjunction + Independent Clause`

### Complex Sentence

A **complex sentence** contains one independent clause and one or more subordinate clauses.

> I passed the exam because I studied.

- `I passed the exam` → Independent clause
- `because I studied` → Subordinate clause

**Structure:**

`Independent Clause + Subordinate Clause`

### Key Difference

| Compound Sentence            | Complex Sentence                      |
|----------------------------- | --------------------------------------|
| Independent + Independent    | Independent + Subordinate             |
| Both clauses can stand alone | Subordinate clause cannot stand alone |
| I studied **and** I passed.  | I passed **because** I studied.       |

---

### 3. Types of Subordinate Clauses

Subordinate clauses are classified according to their **function**.

### A. Noun Clause

A **noun clause** functions like a noun.

It can act as a **subject** or **object**.

> He believed **that his school was intact**.

- `He believed` → Main clause
- `that his school was intact` → Noun clause
- Function → **Object** of `believed`

**Identification:** Ask *what?* or *who?*

> He believed **what?**  
> → that his school was intact.

---

### B. Adjective / Relative Clause

An **adjective clause** modifies a noun or pronoun.

> I met the student **who won the competition**.

The clause `who won the competition` describes the noun `student`.

**Identification:** Ask:

- Which one?
- What kind?
- Which person?

---

### C. Adverbial Clause

An **adverbial clause** provides information about the circumstances of an action or situation.

It can express:

- **Time** → when, while, before, after
- **Condition** → if, unless
- **Reason** → because, since
- **Purpose** → so that
- **Concession** → although, even though
- **Result** → so...that

Example:

> I stayed home **because it was raining**.

`because it was raining` explains **why** I stayed home.

---
### Key Takeaways

- **Independent clause** → can stand alone.
- **Subordinate clause** → depends on another clause.
- **Compound sentence** → independent + independent.
- **Complex sentence** → independent + subordinate.
- **Noun clause** → behaves like a noun.
- **Adjective clause** → modifies a noun or pronoun.
- **Adverbial clause** → provides information such as time, reason, or condition.


### Describing Sentences and  Clauses

- **Sentence vs. Clause:** While a sentence is an independent structural unit, a clause is often embedded within a larger sentence (the **matrix clause**). A sentence can essentially be considered a type of clause.

- **Embedding:** Complex sentences are formed when one clause is embedded within another, such as when a clause serves as the object of a verb. This structure can even include **embedding within embedding**.

- **Finite vs. Non-finite Clauses:**
  - **Finite clauses** contain tense and agreement.
  - **Non-finite (infinitival) clauses** lack tense and agreement, often using infinitive verbs (e.g., *to eat*). These are typically dependent and cannot stand alone as independent sentences.

- **Functional Roles:** Clauses can function like other parts of speech:
  - **Noun-like:** Acting as subjects or objects.
  - **Adjective-like:** Modifying nouns (e.g., relative clauses).
  - **Adverb-like:** Providing additional information or modification.

## Adjectival (Relative) Clauses

- **Sentence Structure:** A sentence consists of a **subject** and a **predicate**. While simple sentences are common, effective communication often uses larger and more complex structures.

- **Types of Larger Sentences:**
  - **Compound:** Two independent clauses connected by a comma or conjunction such as *and* or *or*.
  - **Complex:** A main (independent) clause containing one or more subordinate clauses.

- **Subordinate Clause Functions:**
  - **Nominal:** Acts as a noun, typically in subject or object position.
  - **Adjectival:** Modifies a noun.
  - **Adverbial:** Provides additional information or context.

### Adjectival Clauses

- **Definition:** Also known as **relative clauses**, adjectival clauses function like adjectives by describing or qualifying a noun.

- **Structural Placement:** Unlike standard adjectives, which commonly precede a noun, adjectival clauses generally appear **to the right of the noun** they modify.

- **Relative Pronouns:** Adjectival clauses often begin with **wh- words** such as *which*, *who*, *where*, and *when*. These function as relative pronouns and connect the clause to the noun being modified.

---


- **Core Sentence Components:** Every English sentence is built around two primary components:
  - **Subject:** The actor or topic of the sentence.
  - **Predicate:** The part of the sentence containing the verb. The **verb** is the most important element of the predicate because it determines the structure of the sentence, such as whether it is transitive or intransitive.
  - **Agreement:** The grammatical relationship between the subject and verb, including features such as **number and person**, which ensures grammatical coherence.

### Complements

- **Definition:** Complements are **structurally indispensable** elements required to complete the meaning of a verb or noun. Removing a required complement can make a sentence incomplete or ungrammatical.

- **Example:**
  > Raju needs **a pen**.

  `a pen` → Complement/object of `needs`

- **Key Rule:** A head (such as a verb or noun) typically allows **one complement**.

### Adjuncts

- **Definition:** Adjuncts are **structurally dispensable** elements that provide additional information such as time, place, or manner.

- **Example:**
  > Raju helped Ramu **in the morning**.

  `in the morning` → Adjunct

- **Key Rule:** A sentence can contain **multiple adjuncts**.

### Positional Hierarchy

- **Proximity to Head:** Complements should be placed **close to the head** they complete.
- **Adjunct Placement:** Adjuncts are more flexible in position but should generally remain **away from the head** so that they do not disrupt the relationship between the head and its complement.
- **Error Prevention:** Placing an adjunct between a head and its complement can produce an unacceptable or ungrammatical sequence.

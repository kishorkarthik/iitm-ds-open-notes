## Figures of Speech & Literary/Rhetorical Devices
- A word or phrase used in a non-literal way to add flair, emphasis, or fresh meaning to language. They help turn ordinary writing into creative, memorable expressions.

### Literary & Rhetorical Devices

|  # | Device              | What it means                                                      | How to identify it                          | Example                                             |
| -: | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
|  1 | **Simile**          | Compares two different things explicitly                           | Uses **like, as, than**, etc.               | *He runs **like** the wind.*                        |
|  2 | **Metaphor**        | Directly compares one thing to another                             | Says one thing **is** another               | *He **is** a lion.*                                 |
|  3 | **Personification** | Gives human qualities to non-human things                          | Non-human thing **acts/feels like a human** | *The wind **whispered**.*                           |
|  4 | **Paradox**         | Apparently contradictory statement containing a deeper truth       | **Whole idea** seems contradictory          | *The more you learn, the less you know.*            |
|  5 | **Oxymoron**        | Combines contradictory words                                       | **Opposite/contradictory words together**   | *Deafening silence.*                                |
|  6 | **Antithesis**      | Places opposing ideas in a balanced grammatical structure          | **Contrast + parallel structure**           | *Speech is silver; silence is golden.*              |
|  7 | **Juxtaposition**   | Places contrasting things close together                           | **Contrast through placement**              | *The mansion stood beside the slum.*                |
|  8 | **Irony**           | Contrasts expectation with reality or intended meaning             | **Expected ≠ actual/meaning**               | A fire station burns down.                          |
|  9 | **Alliteration**    | Repetition of initial consonant sounds                             | Same **starting sound** in nearby words     | *Peter **p**icked **p**eppers.*                     |
| 10 | **Assonance**       | Repetition of vowel sounds                                         | Repeated **vowel sound**                    | *The m**oo**n sh**oo**k the r**oo**f.*              |
| 11 | **Consonance**      | Repetition of consonant sounds                                     | Repeated **consonant sound anywhere**       | *bla**ck**, ro**ck**, tra**ck***                    |
| 12 | **Sibilance**       | Repetition of hissing/fricative sounds                             | Repeated **s, z, sh, zh** sounds            | *The **s**nake **s**lithered **s**ilently.*         |
| 13 | **Pun**             | Wordplay exploiting multiple meanings or similar sounds            | A word/phrase has **two interpretations**   | *A bicycle can't stand because it's **two-tired**.* |
| 14 | **Synecdoche**      | A part represents the whole, or vice versa                         | **Part ↔ whole**                            | *All **hands** on deck.*                            |
| 15 | **Metonymy**        | A thing is represented by something closely associated with it     | **Association**, not part-whole             | *The **Crown** announced the decision.*             |
| 16 | **Anaphora**        | Repetition of a word/phrase at the beginning of successive clauses | Same phrase repeated at the **beginning**   | ***We** will fight. **We** will endure.*            |
| 17 | **Litotes**         | Understatement using negation to express a positive                | **Not + negative/opposite**                 | *He's **not a bad** singer.*                        |
| 18 | **Hyperbole**       | Deliberate exaggeration for emphasis                               | Obviously **exaggerated** statement         | *I've told you a **million** times.*                |
| 19 | **Euphemism**       | Mild/indirect expression replacing something unpleasant            | **Softer wording** for a harsh idea         | *Passed away* → died                                |

## Discourse Markers

- **Defining Discourse**: The professors explain that discourse moves beyond individual sounds, words, and sentences into a coherent structure created when sentences are used in a specific context.
- **What are Discourse Markers?**: These are elements—often called "fillers" or "connectors"—that link ideas, manage the flow of conversation, and provide speakers with time to think.

### Functions of Markers:

| **Function**                 | **Purpose**                                                    | **Common Discourse Markers**                                            | **Example**                                                               |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Opening / Starting**       | Introduce a topic or begin a thought                           | so, first of all, to begin with, well                                   | **First of all**, let us consider the main issue.                         |
| **Adding Information**       | Introduce an additional point                                  | moreover, furthermore, besides, in addition, on top of that             | The plan is affordable. **Moreover**, it is easy to implement.            |
| **Sequencing**               | Organize ideas or events in order                              | first, firstly, next, then, finally, lastly                             | **First**, gather the data. **Then**, analyze it.                         |
| **Giving Examples**          | Introduce an example or illustration                           | for example, for instance, such as, to illustrate                       | Many animals communicate. **For example**, bees use movements to signal.  |
| **Clarifying**               | Make an idea easier to understand                              | in other words, that is, I mean, to put it simply                       | The task is optional. **In other words**, you do not have to complete it. |
| **Contrast**                 | Show opposing or different ideas                               | however, nevertheless, on the other hand, in contrast, at the same time | The task is difficult. **However**, it is achievable.                     |
| **Concession**               | Acknowledge an opposing point                                  | although, admittedly, even so, nevertheless, despite this               | **Admittedly**, the method is expensive. **Even so**, it is effective.    |
| **Cause / Reason**           | Explain why something happens                                  | because, since, as, due to this, owing to                               | The match was cancelled **because** it rained.                            |
| **Result / Consequence**     | Show the outcome of something                                  | therefore, thus, consequently, as a result, hence                       | It rained heavily. **As a result**, the match was cancelled.              |
| **Emphasis**                 | Give extra importance to an idea                               | indeed, in fact, actually, above all, notably                           | **In fact**, the results were better than expected.                       |
| **Expressing Stance**        | Show the speaker's attitude or viewpoint                       | apparently, frankly, personally, honestly, to my knowledge              | **Personally**, I prefer the second approach.                             |
| **Clarifying Understanding** | Check whether the listener understands                         | you see, you know, I mean, do you get what I mean?                      | **You see**, the problem is not the cost but the timing.                  |
| **Reformulating**            | Restate something differently                                  | in other words, rather, more precisely, or rather                       | The plan failed—or **rather**, it was never properly implemented.         |
| **Topic Shift**              | Move to another subject                                        | anyway, by the way, moving on, as for, speaking of                      | **By the way**, have you seen the new schedule?                           |
| **Summarizing**              | Condense the main points                                       | in short, in brief, to sum up, in summary                               | **To sum up**, the proposal has three major advantages.                   |
| **Concluding**               | Signal the end of an argument or discussion                    | overall, all in all, in conclusion, ultimately                          | **Overall**, the advantages outweigh the disadvantages.                   |
| **Filling / Thinking Time**  | Give the speaker time to think or maintain conversational flow | well, um, er, you know, let me see, so                                  | **Well**, I'm not sure how to answer that.                                |


### High value distinctions:

| **Marker type**                | **Think**                             |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| **Moreover / furthermore**     | Add another point                     |
| **However / nevertheless**     | Contrast                              |
| **Therefore / consequently**   | Result                                |
| **Because / since**            | Cause                                 |
| **For example / for instance** | Example                               |
| **In other words**             | Clarification / restatement           |
| **In fact / indeed**           | Emphasis                              |

| **In short / to sum up**       | Summary                               |
| **Overall / all in all**       | Conclusion                            |
| **Well / um / you know**       | Conversational filler / thinking time |

## Rhythm and Pitch

### Rhythm

**Rhythm** is the pattern of **stressed and unstressed syllables** in speech. It gives spoken language its natural flow and helps emphasize important words.

> **Rhythm = pattern of stress and timing**

### Pitch

**Pitch** refers to how **high or low** a person's voice sounds. Changes in pitch can express emotion, attitude, emphasis, or intention.

> **Pitch = highness or lowness of voice**

### Key Difference

| **Rhythm**                                | **Pitch**                               |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Pattern of stressed and unstressed sounds | Highness or lowness of voice            |
| Creates flow and timing                   | Conveys emphasis, emotion, and attitude |

## Intonation

**Intonation** is the pattern of **changes in pitch** across spoken language. It helps convey meaning, emotion, and the speaker's intention beyond the literal words.

### Types of Intonation

| Type        | Pattern | Typical Use                                       |
| ----------- | ------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Rising**  | ↗       | Questions, uncertainty, continuation              |
| **Falling** | ↘       | Statements, certainty, completion                 |
| **Flat**    | →       | Little variation in pitch; neutrality or monotony |

### Functions of Intonation

* **Sentence structure:** Helps distinguish statements from questions.
* **Meaning:** Changing pitch placement can emphasize different parts of a sentence and affect its interpretation.
* **Emotion:** Conveys attitudes such as surprise, sarcasm, shock, or pleasure.
* **Discourse:** Signals whether an idea is continuing or complete.
* **Memory:** Emphasis through intonation can help listeners retain information.

> **Intonation = the movement of pitch that adds meaning, emotion, and intention to speech.**
